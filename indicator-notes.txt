Michael Ascot received a phishing email from an outside sender with an invoice ZIP file. Inside was a shortcut pretending to be a PDF, and opening it ran PowerShell, a Windows command tool, which downloaded Powercat and gave the attacker a reverse shell, meaning remote control of the computer. The attacker then used PowerView, a network scanning tool, to study the company network, connected to a shared folder of financial records, and copied files onto Michael's computer. Finally, the stolen files were packed into a ZIP file and sent out in small pieces through DNS lookups, which are normal internet address requests, to attacker controlled domains.
